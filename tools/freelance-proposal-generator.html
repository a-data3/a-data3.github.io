What goes in a freelance proposal

Most freelance proposals lose the job in the first paragraph by talking about the freelancer. The client is reading to find out whether you understood the problem. Open with their situation in your own words, then move to what you will do about it. A short proposal that answers six questions beats a twelve-page template:

  1. Do you understand the problem? Restate what the client told you on the call, including the part they were embarrassed about. This is the section that wins the work.
  2. What is your plan? The steps, in order, so the client can picture the project running.
  3. What do I get? A list of concrete deliverables rather than adjectives.
  4. How long? A duration or a set of milestones, with what you need from them to hit it.
  5. What does it cost? The price, the payment schedule, and a second option at a lower number.
  6. Why you? Two or three proof points that match this project. Skip the general biography.

Give two options, not one

A single price turns the decision into yes or no. Two options turn it into which one, and a surprising share of clients pick the larger package once they can see what the smaller one leaves out. Put the option you want them to buy first and describe it fully, then offer a reduced version at a lower price. Three options work too, though beyond that the choice gets harder and the reply gets slower.

Put a date on it

Proposals go stale. Your rates move and your calendar fills, so a quote from four months ago is a liability when the client resurfaces. A validity date gives you a clean reason to requote, and it nudges a slow decision along. Thirty days is normal for project work.

Send it, then follow up

Paste the proposal into the body of the email rather than attaching a PDF the client has to download. Say when you will follow up, then do it. Frequently asked questions

How long should a freelance proposal be?

One to two pages for most project work. Long proposals get skimmed, and the sections clients actually read are the problem summary, the price and the timeline. If a procurement process demands more, add appendices rather than padding the main document.

Should I put the price in the proposal or wait?

Put it in. Holding the price back to force a call mostly gets you ignored. If the number is likely to be a shock, show a smaller option next to it so the client has somewhere to go other than away.

Is a proposal the same as a quote or a scope of work?

No. A quote is the pricing breakdown, a scope of work defines the deliverables and exclusions in detail, and a proposal is the persuasive document that carries both. Send the proposal first, then the scope of work and contract once the client agrees.

What do I do when the client goes quiet?

Follow up twice, four or five days apart, and keep each message short. If there is still no answer, send a closing note saying you are releasing the slot. That last message gets a reply more often than the two before it.
