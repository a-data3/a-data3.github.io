What moves a virtual assistant's rate

Specialization matters more than anything else. A general admin VA handling email triage, scheduling, data entry, and travel booking usually sits at the lower end of the market. Once you own a specialty, you stop competing on price with everyone who can use a shared calendar. That specialty might be inbox and operations management (OBM), bookkeeping, social media and content scheduling, podcast or launch management, CRM and funnel setup, real estate transaction coordination, or executive support. Specialized VAs routinely charge 1.5 to 3 times what generalists do.

What clients will pay more for

  • Depth in specific software: knowing tools like ClickUp, Dubsado, HoneyBook, Kajabi, QuickBooks, Shopify, or Meta Business Suite well lets you charge for that expertise rather than for keystrokes.
  • Ownership vs. task-taking: "manage my inbox so I never see spam" is worth far more to a client than "reply to these 20 emails," because they are paying for an outcome rather than a task.
  • Time-zone overlap and responsiveness: being available in real time during a US or client business day earns a premium over asynchronous, next-day work.
  • Managing others: if you coordinate the client's other contractors, VAs, or freelancers, that is team-lead work rather than assistant work, and it should be priced that way.
  • Client-facing trust: anything touching money, clients, or the founder's reputation carries risk, and you can charge for carrying it.

Which pricing model fits VA work

Most virtual assistants start on pure hourly, and it is the model that punishes you most as you improve. The better you get, the faster you finish, so hourly billing shrinks your income as a reward for getting good at your job. It also invites clock-watching and awkward conversations about whether a task "should have taken that long."

The model that fits this work better is the monthly retainer package: a set block of hours (commonly 10, 20, or 40) at a rate slightly below your hourly, in exchange for predictable income for you and priority access for the client. A retainer rewards you for being efficient and steadies your calendar. It also turns one-off clients into recurring revenue you can plan around. Use the calculator to find your true hourly number first, then build packages on top of it. A 20-hour retainer at a modest per-hour discount in exchange for the commitment is a common example.

For defined deliverables with a clear scope, such as a launch, a CRM migration, or an inbox-zero cleanup, quote a flat project or value-based price instead of hours. When your work directly produces revenue (managing a sales pipeline, running a launch), base your fee on that value rather than on the hours at your desk.

The mistakes VAs make most often

The most common error is anchoring your rate to the global offshore market you see on job boards and racing to the bottom against $5–$8/hour listings. That is a different service in a different market, and competing there caps your income permanently. The second mistake, specific to retainers, is letting scope creep swallow your margin. This is the client who booked 10 hours but "just adds one more thing" until you find yourself working 18. Define what a package includes and track your hours honestly, then price in a buffer for the extras that always come up. Raise your rates for new clients regularly and give existing clients notice. Your rate should climb as your specialty deepens rather than stay frozen at the number you nervously picked in year one.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the average virtual assistant rate?

No. Averages hide too much. This calculates the rate you need from your own income goal, costs, and billable hours. Use published averages only as a sanity check against your own number.

How many hours can I really bill?

Most full-time freelancers bill 20–30 hours a week once you subtract sales, admin, and unpaid work. Start with a realistic number, because an optimistic one sets your rate too low.

Should I show clients this hourly rate?

Treat it as your private floor and quote fixed project prices where you can. Clients are paying for outcomes rather than hours.
