Freelance marketing consultants typically bill around $65-150/hour in 2026. Entry-level consultants sit near $50-65, and senior or niche specialists (including fractional CMOs) charge $200-350+/hour. Rates vary widely by region, vertical, and track record.

What moves a marketing consultant's rate

Years of experience matter less to your rate than two other things: how well you know a particular vertical, and whether a client trusts you to own an outcome. A generalist who "does marketing" for any business competes with every freelancer on a marketplace and lands near $65-100/hour. A consultant who has run demand gen for three Series A B2B SaaS companies, or scaled paid social for DTC brands past $10M, can charge $200-350+/hour. The buyer is paying for the mistakes you already made on someone else's budget.

The other lever is where you sit between strategy and execution. Consultants who only build the plan (audits, positioning, channel strategy, quarterly roadmaps) can charge high day rates, but they have to keep selling the next engagement. Consultants who own a number, such as marketing-sourced pipeline or blended CAC, can keep a client for a year or more and justify a retainer well above what the hours alone would add up to. Specialization in one channel (lifecycle/email, SEO, paid media, brand) also pays better than "full-stack marketing," because a client finds it easier to trust depth in one area than a promise to cover everything.

The pricing model that fits this work

Hourly billing is a poor fit for marketing consulting. Use it only for ad-hoc advisory or a first paid diagnostic. The more experienced you are, the faster you reach the insight, so an hourly rate pays you less for exactly the expertise the client is hiring. These models work better:

  • Monthly retainer for ongoing work: fractional CMO leadership, managing a channel, or acting as the outsourced marketing function. In 2026 these commonly run $3,000-8,000/month for growth-stage SMBs and $8,000-25,000/month for embedded fractional CMO engagements. Set a floor for yourself and hold to it.
  • Fixed-fee projects for scoped, finite work: a positioning sprint, a go-to-market plan, a marketing audit, a website messaging overhaul. Price these on the outcome and the risk you take on rather than on hours. Fees typically fall between $2,500 and $50,000.
  • Value or performance-based pricing when you can tie your work to revenue and you trust the client's data. This usually means a base retainer plus a percentage of incremental pipeline, or a milestone bonus.

The mistake to avoid

The mistake that keeps marketing consultants underpaid is pricing on effort instead of outcome, and then giving away the expensive part for free. A consultant charges $95/hour for a "strategy deck." The plan ends up changing how the company acquires customers, the client turns it into seven figures of revenue, and the consultant never sees a share of that value. A related mistake is letting a clean strategy retainer drift into unbilled execution and "quick favors," until you are doing an agency's workload at an advisor's price. Scope the deliverable in writing and say what is out of scope. Put a change-order line in every agreement.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the average marketing consultant rate?

No. Averages hide too much variation to be useful on their own. This calculates the rate you need from your own income goal, costs, and billable hours. Use published averages only as a sanity check against that number.

How many hours can I really bill?

Most full-time freelancers bill 20–30 hours a week once you subtract sales, admin, and unpaid work. Be realistic here. If you enter more hours than you can actually bill, the calculator will set your rate too low.

Should I show clients this hourly rate?

Treat it as your private floor and quote fixed project prices where you can. Clients are paying for an outcome, and a fixed price keeps the conversation on that instead of on your hours.
