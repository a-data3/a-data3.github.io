Freelancing in India

India supplies more online freelance work than any other country. Most of it is paid in dollars or pounds and lands in a rupee bank account, which is why payment rails and tax rules are the two questions Indian freelancers ask most. This page covers both at a structural level, with a rate calculator set to rupees.

The wider picture

The labour force survey for 2025 puts average monthly earnings from self-employment at ₹17,914 for men and ₹6,374 for women. That covers every own-account worker in the country, from a shopkeeper to a software contractor, so it works as a national floor rather than a freelance benchmark.

The Economic Survey 2025-26 reports that about 40 percent of gig and platform workers earn under ₹15,000 a month, and that the gig workforce grew from 7.7 million in 2020-21 to 12 million in 2024-25. The earnings figure comes from an industry association that publishes no method, so treat it as indicative.

Getting paid in India

Payoneer, PayPal and Wise are the three channels most Indian freelancers use, and all of them settle into an Indian bank account. Payoneer gives you virtual USD, GBP and EUR account details that clients can pay like a local transfer, then withdraws to your bank in rupees in two to three days. Wise converts at the mid-market rate and usually settles in one to two days. PayPal is accepted everywhere but has the highest fees and the worst exchange rate of the three. A SWIFT wire from the client's bank works for large invoices and is the most expensive option for small ones. Compare what actually reaches your account with the payment fee comparison.

Registration and tax, at a structural level

  • Tax ID. A PAN is needed to file. GST registration (a GSTIN) becomes mandatory once turnover crosses the threshold below.
  • Income tax. Freelance income is taxed as business or professional income at the normal slab rates. Many eligible professions use the Section 44ADA presumptive scheme: declare 50 percent of gross receipts as profit, no detailed books, for receipts up to ₹75 lakh a year.
  • GST. Registration is mandatory once aggregate turnover passes ₹20 lakh a year (₹10 lakh in some special-category states). Services billed to a foreign client and paid in foreign currency count as an export of services and are zero-rated, so a registered freelancer files a Letter of Undertaking and bills at 0 percent.
  • Filing. Income tax return once a year (ITR-4 under the presumptive scheme), with advance tax paid during the year. GST returns monthly or quarterly if registered.

Official source: Income Tax Department and GST portal. The tax set-aside calculator has a simple mode for any country; its US mode does not apply to India.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need GST registration to work for foreign clients?

Only once your turnover crosses the threshold. Below it, registration is optional. Above it, export of services is zero-rated, so you register, file a Letter of Undertaking, and invoice at 0 percent rather than charging IGST and claiming it back.

What is the 44ADA scheme?

A presumptive taxation option for eligible professions. You declare half of your gross receipts as taxable profit without keeping detailed accounts, for receipts up to ₹75 lakh. Check with the Income Tax Department or a chartered accountant whether your profession qualifies.

Which payment method loses the least?

For most people Wise or Payoneer beat PayPal on total cost once the exchange-rate markup is counted. Put your real figures into the fee comparison tool; the best option depends on the amount and the currency.

Should I invoice in rupees or dollars?

Invoice in the client's currency if they insist, but ask for dollars or the client's currency to be converted by a low-markup service on your side. Whatever you choose, state the currency and the payment method on the invoice.
